Local blood flow is interrupted

The recorded left MCA infarction corresponds to new aphasia and right-sided weakness. Her earlier left-sided weakness belongs to the previous cerebrovascular event. The shaded area is illustrative.

Oxygen delivery has two components

Hb 6.1 g/dL reduces oxygen-carrying capacity even with the documented SpO₂ of 99%. This can add stress to tissue already receiving reduced blood flow from the stroke.

Complications can compound injury

K⁺ 8.3 mmol/L creates cardiac electrical risk. An arrhythmia could impair brain perfusion, but neither an arrhythmia nor dialysis-related hypotension is documented in the supplied case.

Case evidence and points to verify before presenting
  • Patient source: 9TH MW_CASE PRES (1).pdf — Patient Profile/History, Physical Assessment, Laboratory and Diagnostic Exams, Medical Management, Course in the Ward and Discharge Plan.
  • Motor findings: physical assessment lists right 0/5 and left 2/5; the management table reverses the sides. This model follows the narrative of new right-sided weakness and previous left-sided weakness, pending chart verification.
  • Updated ward timeline: HD dates differ between the narrative (Sept 17/19/20) and dated entries (Sept 18/21). Seizure timing differs (Sept 19 evening versus Sept 20 early morning). Epoetin frequency, statin identity, ARB use, diazepam maximum, valproate formulation and antibiotic sequence need reconciliation; see Medical management above.
  • Timing: the principal diagnosis lists September 15, 2026 at 7 AM; the history also describes evening recognition. Verify last-known-well and symptom recognition time.
  • Unconfirmed factors: missed HD, high potassium intake, diabetes, dyslipidemia, genetic predisposition, a specific clot source, facial droop and visual-field loss must not be presented as documented patient findings.
  • Flowchart correction: hyperkalemia should lead to electrical/conduction risk, not directly to atherosclerosis or an MCA clot. Label the renal hormone pathway “decreased erythropoietin production.”
  • Outcome: improved GCS does not prove complete motor/language recovery; the supplied follow-up potassium and hemoglobin values remain abnormal. The actual CT report, ECG findings and final discharge laboratory values are not included.

Interpretation: CKD is a background vascular risk state. This model does not establish the cause of the MCA occlusion, imply that hyperkalemia causes a cerebral clot, or assume missed dialysis. Only findings explicitly identified from the case are presented as documented. Other mechanisms remain possible explanations. The highlighted region is not a quantitative infarct map. Flow particles show direction only, not measured flow, potassium concentration or blood-cell count.
